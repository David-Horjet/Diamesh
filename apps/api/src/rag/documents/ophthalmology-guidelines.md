# Ophthalmology Clinical Guidelines — Diamesh Knowledge Base

## Diabetic Retinopathy

### Classification (ETDRS)
- **Mild NPDR**: Microaneurysms only
- **Moderate NPDR**: More than mild, less than severe. Dot-blot hemorrhages, hard exudates, cotton wool spots.
- **Severe NPDR**: 4-2-1 rule — hemorrhages in 4 quadrants, venous beading in 2 quadrants, or intraretinal microvascular abnormalities (IRMA) in 1 quadrant.
- **PDR (Proliferative)**: Neovascularization of disc (NVD) or elsewhere (NVE). High risk: NVD ≥ 1/4 to 1/3 DA, any NVD with vitreous hemorrhage, NVE ≥ 1/2 DA with vitreous hemorrhage.

### Diabetic Macular Edema (DME)
- Clinically Significant Macular Edema (CSME): Thickening or hard exudates within 500μm of foveal centre, or retinal thickening ≥ 1 disc area within 1 DA of centre.
- Anti-VEGF (ranibizumab, aflibercept, bevacizumab) is first-line for centre-involving DME.
- OCT is gold standard for detecting and monitoring DME.

### Screening Intervals
- Type 1 DM: First exam 5 years after diagnosis, then annually
- Type 2 DM: At diagnosis, then annually
- Pregnancy with pre-existing DM: First trimester, then each trimester

### Treatment
- Mild-moderate NPDR: Observation, optimise systemic control
- Severe NPDR: Consider panretinal photocoagulation (PRP) or anti-VEGF
- PDR: PRP, anti-VEGF, or vitrectomy if vitreous haemorrhage/TRD

## Glaucoma

### Primary Open-Angle Glaucoma (POAG)
- Defined by: characteristic optic nerve damage + visual field defect, with open anterior chamber angle. IOP may be normal or elevated.
- Risk factors: elevated IOP, age >60, African descent, family history, thin CCT (<520μm), myopia.
- Target IOP: typically 30% reduction from baseline, or <18 mmHg; adjust based on damage severity.

### Diagnosis
- Gonioscopy: essential to classify angle
- Visual field testing (Humphrey/Goldmann): detect and monitor damage
- OCT RNFL: structural assessment, detects damage before functional loss
- IOP measurement (Goldmann applanation is gold standard)
- CCT measurement: thinner corneas underestimate true IOP

### Treatment Ladder
1. Prostaglandin analogues (latanoprost, bimatoprost): first-line, once daily dosing
2. Beta-blockers (timolol): effective but systemic side effects
3. CAIs (dorzolamide/brinzolamide) ± alpha-agonists (brimonidine)
4. Laser: SLT (selective laser trabeculoplasty) can be first-line
5. Surgery: trabeculectomy, tube shunt, MIGS for refractory cases

### Acute Angle-Closure Glaucoma (AACG) — Emergency
- Symptoms: severe eye pain, headache, nausea, halos, blurred vision, raised IOP (>40 mmHg)
- Treatment: IV acetazolamide, topical IOP-lowering drops, laser peripheral iridotomy (LPI)
- Refer immediately

## Age-Related Macular Degeneration (AMD)

### Classification
- **Early AMD**: Small drusen (<63μm), no pigmentary changes
- **Intermediate AMD**: Medium drusen (63-124μm) or large drusen, pigmentary abnormalities
- **Late AMD**:
  - Dry (Geographic Atrophy): RPE atrophy, slow central vision loss
  - Wet (Neovascular): CNV, rapid vision loss possible

### Risk Factors
- Age (>75 strongest risk), smoking (2-4x increased risk), family history, Caucasian race, cardiovascular disease, obesity.

### Investigation
- OCT: essential for detecting fluid (wet AMD), monitoring treatment response
- Fundus fluorescein angiography (FFA): characterise CNV lesion
- Amsler grid: home monitoring for metamorphopsia

### Treatment
- Dry AMD: No proven treatment. AREDS2 supplements (vitamins C, E, lutein, zeaxanthin, zinc, copper) for intermediate/advanced AMD reduce progression by ~25%.
- Wet AMD: Anti-VEGF (ranibizumab, aflibercept, brolucizumab) — monthly or treat-and-extend regimen.

## Retinal Detachment

### Types
- **Rhegmatogenous** (most common): break in retina allowing fluid entry. Symptoms: new floaters, flashes (photopsia), curtain/shadow in vision.
- **Tractional**: fibrovascular proliferation pulls retina. Common in PDR.
- **Exudative**: subretinal fluid without break, from inflammation/tumour.

### Management
- Immediate referral for any suspected retinal detachment
- Surgery: pneumatic retinopexy, scleral buckle, or pars plana vitrectomy (PPV)
- Macula-on detachment: surgical emergency (<24h)
- Macula-off: surgery within 7-10 days (every day matters for visual outcome)

## Cornea

### Keratoconus
- Progressive corneal ectasia, usually bilateral asymmetric. Onset typically teens to 30s.
- Signs: Munson's sign, Fleischer ring, Vogt's striae, inferior steepening on topography.
- Management: spectacles (early), rigid gas-permeable lenses, corneal cross-linking (CXL) to halt progression, DALK or PK for advanced cases.

### Dry Eye Disease
- Multifactorial disease of tears and ocular surface. Two main subtypes: aqueous-deficient (Sjögren's, lid disease) and evaporative (meibomian gland dysfunction, MGD).
- Symptoms: burning, foreign body sensation, watering (reflex), blurred vision, photophobia.
- TFOS DEWS II management: lid hygiene, lubricants, dietary omega-3, anti-inflammatory drops (cyclosporine, lifitegrast), punctal plugs, intense pulsed light (IPL).

## Visual Acuity Reference

### Snellen Notation
- 6/6 (20/20): Normal
- 6/9 (20/30): Mild reduction
- 6/18 (20/60): Moderate — driving threshold in many countries
- 6/60 (20/200): Severe — legal blindness threshold in US
- CF (counting fingers), HM (hand movements), PL (perception of light), NPL (no perception of light)

### Pinhole Test
- Improvement with pinhole suggests refractive error as the cause
- No improvement suggests media opacity or retinal/neural pathology

## Optic Nerve

### Optic Neuritis
- Typically unilateral, associated with multiple sclerosis (MS).
- Presents with painful visual loss, RAPD, dyschromatopsia (red desaturation).
- IV methylprednisolone speeds recovery but does not improve final VA.
- 50% risk of developing MS within 15 years.

### Papilledema
- Bilateral optic disc swelling from raised ICP. Causes: idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH), space-occupying lesion, venous sinus thrombosis.
- IIH: typically young overweight females, headache, transient visual obscurations, pulsatile tinnitus.
- Urgent neuroimaging and lumbar puncture required.
- Management: weight loss, acetazolamide, optic nerve sheath fenestration, LP shunting.
